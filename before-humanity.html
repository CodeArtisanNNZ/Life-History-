Before the human story · revelation track

Angels, jinn & the rebellion of Iblis

This chapter uses the Qur'an and authentic/accepted hadith as its primary basis. It deliberately separates what revelation actually states from later folklore, popular storytelling and Isra'iliyyat.

Angels · الملائكة

Created from light and completely obedient to Allah

Sahih Muslim states that the angels were created from light. The Qur'an describes them as honored servants who do not disobey Allah and who carry out what they are commanded. The Qur'an also describes angels as messengers with wings.

Sahih Muslim 2996 ↗ Qur'an 66:6 ↗ Qur'an 35:1 ↗
Jinn · الجن

Created from smokeless fire and morally responsible

The Qur'an says the jinn were created from a smokeless flame of fire and existed before Adam. Unlike angels, jinn can obey or disobey. Surah al-Jinn describes believing jinn, righteous jinn, and others who are unjust or follow different paths.

Qur'an 55:15 ↗ Qur'an 15:27 ↗ Qur'an 72:11 ↗ Qur'an 72:14 ↗

The four major angels commonly taught in Islamic studies

This “main four” grouping is a common teaching framework rather than a Qur'anic list titled “the four main angels.” The cards below distinguish direct primary-source evidence from later scholarly assignment.

01

Jibril

جبريل

Jibril is the angel of revelation. Qur'an 2:97 explicitly says that Jibril brought the Qur'an down to the Prophet's heart by Allah's permission.

Primary duty: carrying revelation to Allah's prophets and messengers.
Qur'an 2:97 ↗
02

Mika'il

ميكائيل

Mika'il is named in Qur'an 2:98 and appears with Jibril in authentic hadith. Sahih Muslim reports Jibril and Mika'il beside the Prophet ﷺ at Uhud.

Commonly taught duty: rain, vegetation and provision.
Source boundary: that detailed assignment is well known in classical scholarship, but it is not spelled out directly in the Qur'an or in the wording of Sahih Muslim 770 itself.
Qur'an 2:98 ↗Sahih Muslim 2306a ↗
03

Israfil

إسرافيل

Israfil is named in authentic hadith alongside Jibril and Mika'il. The Qur'an repeatedly describes the Trumpet being blown at the end of the world and for resurrection.

Duty: the angel entrusted with the Trumpet, awaiting Allah's command to blow it.
Sahih Muslim 770 ↗Qur'an 39:68 ↗
04

Malak al-Mawt

ملك الموت

The Qur'an calls him the Angel of Death and says that he is entrusted with taking souls before people return to their Lord.

Duty: taking souls at death by Allah's command.
The name “Azrael / ʿIzra'il” is popular in later tradition, but the Qur'an and the primary authentic sources used here call him Malak al-Mawt, not Azrael.
Qur'an 32:11 ↗

The actual primary-source story

Iblis: from jinn, not a fallen angel

The Qur'an is explicit that Iblis was from the jinn. His story is about pride, refusal and rebellion—not an angel becoming evil.

1

Jinn already existed before Adam. The Qur'an says the jinn were created before humankind from scorching/smokeless fire.

2

Ajajil / Azazil in later Islamic tradition. Some later tafsir and storytelling traditions say that before his rebellion, Iblis was known as Ajajil / Azazil / ʿAzāzīl and had been a devoted worshipper. This is included here as later tradition, not as a Qur'anic or authentic-hadith fact.

3

Adam is created. Allah commands the angels to prostrate to Adam. They obey.

4

Iblis refuses. Qur'an 18:50 identifies Iblis as a jinn. He refuses the command and rebels against his Lord.

5

His reason is arrogance. He argues that fire is superior to clay and says he is better than Adam.

6

He asks for respite. Iblis asks to remain until the Day people are resurrected, and he is granted respite until the appointed time.

7

He vows to mislead humanity. He promises to approach people from different directions, but the Qur'an makes clear that he has no coercive authority over Allah's sincere servants.

18:50 · Iblis was jinn ↗ 7:11–18 · refusal & arrogance ↗ 15:26–44 · creation & respite ↗ 38:71–85 · fire vs clay claim ↗
Ajajil / Azazil / ʿAzāzīl — the traditional former name associated with Iblis: In later Islamic storytelling and some tafsir traditions, Iblis is sometimes described as having been called Ajajil (also written Azazil / ʿAzāzīl) before his rebellion. In that traditional account, he had worshipped Allah for a long time and was present among the angels when Adam was created; after refusing Allah's command to prostrate to Adam out of pride, he became known as Iblis and was cast away.

Source boundary: this former-name story is not stated in the Qur'an and is not established by an authentic hadith. The Qur'an itself calls him Iblis, explicitly identifies him as one of the jinn in 18:50, and gives the confirmed story of his refusal, arrogance and rebellion. So Life History includes the Ajajil/Azazil account as later Islamic tradition, not as confirmed revelation.

Types and categories of jinn actually supported by the sources

The Qur'an does not give a fantasy-style species chart. It describes moral categories and some specific descriptions. These are the safest categories to teach from primary Islamic sources.

Moral category

Muslim / believing jinn

Surah al-Jinn says that among the jinn are Muslims who have deliberately sought the right path.

72:14 ↗
Moral category

Righteous jinn

The jinn themselves say that some among them are righteous while others are not, and that they follow different ways.

72:11 ↗
Moral category

Unjust / disbelieving jinn

Surah al-Jinn contrasts the Muslims with unjust jinn and warns of their punishment.

72:14–15 ↗
Rebellious devil

Shayatin

“Shaytan” is not a name for every jinn. It describes rebellious devils; the Qur'an even speaks of devils among both jinn and humans. Iblis is the central jinn rebel in the Qur'anic story.

6:112 ↗18:50 ↗
Description

Ifrit

Qur'an 27:39 mentions an ifrit from the jinn offering Sulayman extraordinary strength and speed. The verse presents “ifrit” as a powerful jinn description.

27:39 ↗
Qarin

Sahih Muslim states that every person has a companion from the jinn. This is a role/relationship, not proof of a separate biological “species” of jinn.

Sahih Muslim 2814a ↗
What about “marid,” ghoul, si'lat, hinn, etc.?

Popular charts often turn folklore words into fixed jinn species. The Qur'an uses words such as marid in the sense of a rebellious devil, but that does not establish a zoological-style species taxonomy. This site will not present folklore classifications as revealed fact.

Qur'an 37:7 ↗
Key distinction: angels obey Allah and are not jinn. Jinn have moral choice. Iblis is a jinn, not an angel. “Shaytan” describes rebellion and evil, while “jinn” names the broader created kind.
